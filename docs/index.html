02 · AI ALLOWANCE

“Yes, but”: AI is accepted, on conditions

Nearly every policy that allows AI asks for something in return, even if only that you understand the code you submit. Outright bans are a minority.

AI allowance at a glance

AI allowance in the 1,284 policies

AI allowance by popularity rank

  • In favor
  • Against
  • Neutral
  • Undetermined

In favor, no condition accepts AI with no extra request. In favor, with conditions asks for anything at all: disclosure, understanding the code, review, no autonomous agents. Against, with exceptions bans or discourages AI but allows narrow uses such as translation. Neutral regulates only PR text, volume or attribution.

03 · THE HUMAN

Policies put a human in charge

Three in four policies that accept AI set duties for the human, often under the name “human in the loop”: understand and explain the change, own it, review and test what the tool produced, and speak for yourself in reviews.

05 · COUNTERMEASURES AGAINST AI SLOP

How projects defend their review time

The first line of defense is accountability: disclose AI use and keep autonomous agents out. Enforcement follows from closing without review to public bans. Newer and rarer are barriers to entry and traps aimed at agents.

METHODOLOGY

  • Sample. The 25,000 most-starred public GitHub repositories (forks excluded), collected on 25 November 2026. One repository could not be downloaded.
  • Files. CONTRIBUTING.md at the root, .github/ and docs/, plus AI_POLICY.md and AI_USAGE_POLICY.md. Policies kept only on a website, a wiki or in AGENTS.md, with no mention in these files, are not counted, so adoption is a lower bound.
  • Classification. Claude Opus 5.5 was used to classify the policies. Where a count starts from text patterns, the patterns only found candidate sentences; every candidate was then read, and false matches were removed. Policies with unusual wording may still be missed, so those counts are a lower bound. An AI model can misread or misclassify a policy, so treat the figures as estimates.
  • 01 · Adoption of AI policies. A policy is a rule about using AI to contribute, found in CONTRIBUTING.md or an AI_POLICY.md file. Every candidate file was read; projects that only describe their own AI features or agent tooling were removed as false positives. Popularity tiers are ranks by star count. Language is GitHub’s detected primary language.
  • 02 · AI allowance. Each policy was read and placed in one category. Ambiguous cases were reread in full, and policies that only link elsewhere were read at the linked page or file.
  • 03 · The human. Counted only among policies that accept AI. A duty counts when the policy asks it of the contributor, not of maintainers.
  • 04 · AI disclosure. Where and how strongly disclosure is asked was classified for each policy. What to include, and each mention of a git trailer (labelled as asked for or forbidden), were found by text patterns and then read.
  • 05 · Countermeasures against AI slop. Disclosure comes from section 04; the other mechanisms were found by text patterns and then read.
